On December 2, 2024, Beijing Shangye Film and Television Culture Media Co. LRD ("Petitioner") filed
a petition to enforce foreign arbitration ("Petition") against UCFTI, INC. and Zuo Hong Chen
("Respondents"). ECF Docket No. ("Dkt.") 1. The Petition seeks to confirm the December 7, 2021
award decision (the "Award") by the Beijing Arbitration Commission ("BAC") against Respondents.
See id. On December 28, 2024, Respondents filed an answer. Dkt. 10. Respondents filed a corrected
answer on January 13, 2025. Dkt. 12.
On January 20, 2025, the parties filed a stipulation to strike Respondents' answer in its entirety
because, as a suspended corporation, the UCFTI was prevented by law from defending against this
action. Dkt. 14. The Court granted the stipulation on January 27, 2025. Dkt. 16. On the same day, the
clerk entered a default against UCFTI. Dkt. 18.
On February 27, 2025, Petitioner filed a motion for default judgment ("Motion") against UCFTI. Dkt.
17. Respondents have not filed an Opposition to the Motion. The Court finds this matter appropriate
for resolution without oral argument. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 78(b); L.R. 7-15. For the reasons stated below,
both the Petition and Motion are GRANTED
GRANTED.

Sections 203 and 207 of Title 9 of the United States Code provide jurisdiction in federal district courts
for cases arising under the Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral
Awards of June 10, 1958. 9 U.S.C. §§ 203, 207; see also 9 U.S.C. § 201 (codifying the Convention).
"Within three years after an arbitral award falling under the Convention is made, any party to the

arbitration may apply to any court.. .for an order confirming the award as against any other party
to the arbitration." 9 U.S.C. § 207.
"Confirmation under the Convention is a summary proceeding in nature, which is not intended to
involve complex factual determinations, other than a determination of the limited statutory
conditions for confirmation or grounds for refusal to confirm." Castro v. Tri Marine Fish Company
LLC, 921 F.3d 766, 773 (9th Cir. 2019) (quoting Zeiler v. Deitsch, 500 F.3d 157, 169 (2d Cir. 2007)).
Accordingly, the Court's review of a foreign arbitration award "is quite circumscribed." China Nat'l
Metal Prods. Import/Export Co. v. Apex Digital, Inc., 379 F.3d 796, 799 (9th Cir. 2004). The Court does
not review the merits of the underlying arbitration, but merely whether the party established a
defense under the Convention, and "[a] court must confirm a foreign arbitral award unless the party
resisting enforcement meets its 'substantial' burden of proving one of seven narrowly interpreted
defenses." Castro, 921 F.3d at 773 (quoting Polimaster Ltd. V. RAE Sys., Inc., 623 F.3d 832, 836 (9th Cir.
2010)).
"The seven grounds for refusing to confirm an award are set out in Article V of the Convention."
Ministry of Defense and Support for the Armed Forces of the Islamic Republic of Iran v. Cubic
Defense Systems, Inc., 665 F.3d 1091, 1096 (9th Cir. 2011). Specifically, the seven defenses are:
(1) the parties to the agreement were under some incapacity or the agreement was invalid under
law;
(2) the party against whom the award is invoked was not given proper notice of the appointment or
the arbitration proceedings or was otherwise unable to present his case;
(3) the award deals with a difference not contemplated by or not falling within the terms of the
submission to arbitration, or it contains decisions on matters beyond the scope of the submission to
arbitration;
(4) the composition of the arbitral authority or the arbitral procedure was not in accordance with
the agreement of the parties, or, failing such agreement, was not in accordance with the law of the
country where the arbitration took place;
(5) the award has not yet become binding on the parties, or has been set aside or suspended by a
competent authority of the country in which, or under the law of which, that award was made;
(6) the subject matter of the difference is not capable of settlement by arbitration under the law of
that country; and
(7) the recognition or enforcement of the award would be contrary to the public policy of that
country.
See 21 U.S.T. 2517 art. V, §§ 1(a)-(e), 2(a)-(b).

The Court finds that the petition should be granted and the award against UCFTI should be
confirmed.
First, the Award was issued on December 7, 2021. Dkt. 1 at 59-74. Petitioner filed the instant action
on December 2, 2024, i.e., within three years after the Award was issued, and thus, the action is
timely. See Dkt. 1. Therefore, pursuant to 9 U.S.C. §§ 203 and 207, the Court has jurisdiction to
confirm the Award.
Second, Respondent UCFTI has failed to respond to the Petition or file an opposition to the Motion
and, thus, fails to meet its burden of establishing one of the seven defenses provided by Article V of
the Convention is applicable. Moreover, the Court independently finds the defenses inapplicable.
The first defense is inapplicable because no evidence exists to support a finding that UCFTI was
incapacitated or the agreement was invalid under law. The second defense is inapplicable because
UCFTI was on notice regarding the December 7, 2021 arbitration proceedings; indeed, UCFTI
participated in the proceedings. Dkt. 1 at 67 ("The agents of the Applicant and the Respondent I
[UCFTI] attended this court hearting [the Arbitral Tribunal]."). The third defense is inapplicable
because the arbitrator based his decision on the parties' Film Distribution Right Investment
Contract ("Contract"), which stipulates that the parties shall submit to the Beijing Arbitration
Commission to resolve any disputes. Id. at 23-32, 63. The fourth defense is inapplicable because the
arbitration was "conducted according to Chapter VIII Rules on International Commercial
Arbitration of the Arbitration Rules of Beijing Arbitration Commission," as stipulated to by the
parties in the Contract. See id. at 63. The fifth defense is inapplicable because the Award was issued
on December 7, 2021, and thus, is binding. See id. at 59-74. The sixth defense is inapplicable because
this action involves a contract dispute, and courts have held contract disputes are capable of
settlement by arbitration. See, e.g., TMCO Ltd. v. Green Light Energy Solutions R&D Corp., No.
4:17-cv-00997-KAW, 2017 WL 5450762, at *4 (N.D. Cal. Nov. 14, 2017) (confirming an arbitral award
made in London arising from a contract dispute); BU8 Sdn. Bhd. v. CreAgri, Inc., No. C-14-4503-EMC,
2015 WL 1010090, at *9 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 6, 2015) (confirming an arbitral award made in Singapore
arising from a contract dispute). The seventh defense is inapplicable because no evidence exists to
support a finding that confirming the Award would be contrary to public policy. See Consim Info
Pvt. Ltd. v. Burning Glass Int'l, Inc., No. 11-1053-IEG-MDDx, 2011 WL 13356183, at *3 (S.D. Cal. Nov.
8, 2011) (finding enforcement of a foreign arbitral award related to a standard commercial contract
dispute would not be contrary to public policy).
Lastly, as mentioned previously, Respondent UCFTI has not filed an opposition to the Motion or
asserted any other challenges to enforcement. Thus, confirming the Award will not run afoul of the
seven defenses provided by Article V of the Convention. Therefore, having jurisdiction and
Respondent UCFTI having failed to meet its burden of establishing a defense under Article V of the
Convention applies, the Court GRANTS
GRANTS the Petition.

1.1.  Applicable
Applicable  law
law
The decision to grant or deny an application for default judgment under Fed. R. Civ. P. 55(b)(2) is
discretionary. Allstate Life Ins. Co. v. Markowitz, 590 F. Supp. 3d 1210, 1215 (C.D. Cal. 2022) (citing
Aldabe v. Aldabe, 616 F.2d 1089, 1092 (9th Cir. 1980)). In determining whether to enter default
judgment against a party, a court considers the following factors: (1) the possibility of prejudice to
the plaintiff, (2) the merits of the plaintiff's substantive claims, (3) the sufficiency of the complaint,
(4) the sum of money at stake in the action, (5) the possibility of a dispute concerning material facts,
(6) whether the default was due to excusable neglect, and (7) the strong policy underlying the
Federal Rules of Civil Procedure favoring decisions on the merits. Eitel v. McCool, 782 F.2d 1470,
1471-72 (9th Cir. 1986).
Under this Court's Local Rules, an application for default judgment must be accompanied by a
declaration setting forth: when, and against what party, default was entered; the pleading to which
default was entered; whether the defaulting party is an infant or incompetent person; that the
Servicemembers Civil Relief Act, 50 U.S.C. § 3931, does not apply; and that notice has been served on
the defaulting party, if required. L.R. 55-1. The party applying for default judgment must also
provide the defaulting party with notice of the application if the defaulting party has appeared in
the action or the applicant seeks unliquidated damages. Fed. R. Civ. P. 55(b)(2); L.R. 55-2.
The Court need not make detailed findings of fact in the event of a default judgment. Adriana Int'l
Corp. v. Thoeren, 913 F.2d 1406, 1414 (9th Cir. 1990). "The general rule of law is that upon default the
factual allegations of the complaint, except those relating to the amount of damages, will be taken
as true." Geddes v. United Fin. Grp., 559 F.2d 557, 560 (9th Cir. 1977); see also Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(b)(6)
("An allegation—other than one relating to the amount of damages—is admitted if a responsive
pleading is required and the allegation is not denied. If a responsive pleading is not required, an
allegation is considered denied or avoided."). Allegations as to the amount of damages must be
proven. TeleVideo Sys., Inc. v. Heidenthal, 826 F.2d 915, 917-18 (9th Cir. 1987). Furthermore, "a
'defendant is not held to admit facts that are not well-pleaded or to admit conclusions of law.'"
DIRECTV, Inc. v. Huynh, 503 F.3d 847, 854 (9th Cir. 2007) (quoting Nishimatsu Constr. Co. v. Houston
Nat'l Bank, 515 F.2d 1200, 1206 (5th Cir. 1975)).
2.2.  Analysis
Analysis
As a preliminary matter, Petitioner presents a declaration which adequately addresses the
requirements set forth in the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the Court's Local Rules. Petitioner
has submitted a declaration that complies with Local Rule 55-1 and affirms that notice of the
application is not required under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 55(b)(2) and Local Rule 55-1
because UCFTI did not appear in this action. See Dkt. 17-2, Declaration of Kaisheng Yang ("Yang
Decl."), ¶ 7. Additionally, Respondent UCFTI is a corporation and, therefore, is not a minor, an
incompetent person, or currently a member of the military service. Id. ¶ 8.
In addition, the Court finds the seven Eitel factors weigh in favor of entering default judgment. The
first factor favors entry of default judgment because Respondent has failed to respond to the instant

Petition, thus leaving Petitioner with no other recourse for recovery other than default judgment.
See Amini Innovation Corp. v. KTY Int'l Mktg., 768 F. Supp. 2d 1049, 1056 (C.D. Cal. 2011) (finding
prejudice to plaintiff favored entering default judgment because, "[i]f the Court does not enter a
default judgment, it will allow [the defendant] to avoid liability by not responding to [the plaintiff's]
claims"). The second and third factors favor entry of default judgment because Petitioner has
sufficiently alleged a contractual relationship and dispute between it and Respondent UCFTI;
presented a foreign arbitral award against Respondent in favor of Petitioner; and the Court finds the
Petition should be granted. In addition, Respondent UCFTI has not filed an opposition to the Motion.
The fourth factor favors entry of default judgment because Petitioner is seeking only the amount
of monies and damages owed pursuant to the Contract. See Dkt. 1 at 25. The fifth factor favors
entry of default judgment because, in light of the apparent unwillingness of UCFTI to participate
further in this matter or challenge the allegations in the Petition, the possibility of any dispute as
to material facts is remote. See Wecosign, Inc. v. IFG Holdings, Inc., 845 F. Supp. 2d 1072, 1083 (C.D.
Cal. 2012) (finding "possibility of dispute concerning material facts" diminished due to defendants'
"subsequent failure to contest [pjlaintiff's claims"). The sixth factor favors entry of default judgment
because Respondent participated in the arbitration proceedings, initially appeared in this action,
and yet failed to file a responsive pleading or oppose this application. See Primerica Life Ins. Co. v.
Annalyn Ngo, CV 23-1478-JLS-AFMx, 2023 WL 7308310 at *4 (C.D. Cal. Oct. 20, 2023) (finding sixth
factor favored entry of default judgment due to defendant being served and thus aware of litigation,
making it less likely defendant's failure to litigate was a product of excusable neglect); see also
Craigslist, Inc. v. Naturemarket, Inc., 694 F.Supp.2d 1039, 1061 (N.D. Cal. 2010) (finding evidence that
defendants were "clearly aware of the pending litigation" rendered it unlikely their failure to litigate
resulted from excusable neglect and favored entering default judgment). Finally, while the seventh
factor, the policy favoring decisions on the merits, "always weighs against default judgment," Amini
Innovation Corp., 768 F. Supp. 2d at 1056, this factor, "standing alone, is not dispositive." PepsiCo,
Inc. v. Cal. Sec. Cans, 238 F. Supp. 2d 1172, 1177 (C.D. Cal. 2002). Here, the apparent unwillingness
of Respondent to participate in this action "makes a decision on the merits impractical, if not
impossible." See id. Thus, the policy favoring decisions on the merits does not preclude entering
default judgment. See id.; see also Wecosign, Inc., 845 F. Supp. 2d at 1083 (finding default judgment
appropriate when "a decision on the merits is impossible").
Ultimately, the Court finds all seven relevant factors weigh in favor of entering default judgment.
Accordingly, the Court GRANTS
GRANTS Petitioner's Motion for Default Judgment.
