Based on the evidence and statements submitted by the Parties, the emergency arbitrator
understands the transactions and background to the dispute related to the emergency arbitration
procedure as follows:
HK Company X and HK Company Y are companies registered in Hong Kong, and the legal
representative of the two companies is Malaysian national Mr. Gu. Cayman Islands Company Z is
registered in the Cayman Islands. Mr. R is a national of Mainland China and is the actual controller
of Cayman Islands Company Z.
On 20 August 2012, the Applicants signed Investment Agreements with Cayman Islands Company Z,
shareholder of Cayman Islands Company Z, Mr. R, and seven other shareholders. The Investment
Agreements provide that HK Company X and HK Company Y respectively invest US$14,716,346 to
Cayman Islands Company Z and acquired a 10% stake in Cayman Islands Company Z. On 28 August
2012, the Applicants fulfilled their capital investment obligations to Cayman Islands Company Z and
acquired a 10% stake in Cayman Islands Company Z.
Article 4.2.1 of the Investment Agreements stipulate that if the aggregated profits of Cayman Islands
Company Z and its Shenzhen affiliated Company S is less than the "guaranteed net profit", the
Applicants have the right to require Cayman Islands Company Z, Mr. R, and seven other
shareholders to increase the shareholding ratios of the Applicants in Cayman Islands Company Z
each to 12.5%.
Article 4.2.2 of the Investment Agreements stipulate that before Cayman Islands Company Z or
Shenzhen Company S is publicly listed in accordance with Article 5.1 of the Investment Agreements,
the annual consolidated net profit of Cayman Islands Company Z and Shenzhen Company S shall
not be less than "the guaranteed minimum annual net profit", otherwise the Applicants have the
right to either require Cayman Islands Company Z and Mr. R to make up for the profit or to
undertake the repurchase obligation.
Article 5.1 of the Investment Agreements stipulate that Cayman Islands Company Z or Shenzhen
Company S will have its initial public offerings in domestic or internationally recognized stock
exchange and be listed for transaction before 31 December 2017. The specific plan for listing will be
decided by the resolution of the company's shareholders taking into account of market situations
and the company's performance.

The Applicants submitted that the conditions for repurchase agreed in the Investment Agreements
had been fulfilled:
1.
According to Article 6.1 (10) of the Investment Agreements, if any of the annual minimum
net profit of Cayman Islands Company Z and Shenzhen Company S is not met in any one
year before the listing, the Applicants shall have the right to request the Respondents to
perform their repurchase obligations. The Applicants argued that the minimum net profit
of the Cayman Islands Company Z from 2014 to 2016 failed to meet the minimum net profit
specified in Section 4.2.2 of the Investment Agreements, which triggered the repurchase
obligation by the Respondents in accordance with Article 6.1 (10) of the Investment
Agreement.
2.
According to Article 6.1 (15) of the Investment Agreements, if the Cayman Islands Company
Z conducts transactions or guarantees with its affiliates that are detrimental to the investor,
the Applicants have the right to request the Applicants to assume the repurchase
obligation. The Applicants claimed that Cayman Islands Company Z did not distribute the
shares to shareholders according to the resolutions of the shareholders' meeting made on
18 September 2015 after obtaining the shares of the company. Instead, Cayman Islands
Company Z pledged the shares to the Securities Companies, and loaned the money financed
to HK Capital Company M. The Respondents' aforementioned acts transformed the stock
assets that are highly liquid and can be liquidated by the shareholders at any time
according to market situations into illiquid assets, which triggered the repurchase
obligation of the Respondents in accordance with Article 6.1 (15) of the Investment
Agreement.
3.
According to Article 6.1 of the Investment Agreements, in the event of any of circumstances
stipulated in this Article, the Applicants shall have the right to request the Respondents to

(1) The effect of the repurchase clause:
First, China's Company Law does not completely prohibit companies from repurchasing shares.
Article 142 of the Company Law stipulates that the company may not acquire shares of its own.
However, there are several exceptions to the rule: (1) when the company intends to reduce
its registered capital; (2) when the company intends to merge with other companies holding
shares of the company; (3) when the company intends to reward its employees with shares of
the company; (4) when the shareholders hold objections to the Resolution of the Shareholders'
Meetings on the merger and division of the company, and requires the company to repurchase
its shares in the company. Although Article 6.1 (10) of the Investment Agreements does not
directly fall into the following four situations, once the repurchase obligation is fulfilled, it
will objectively result in a decrease in the company's registered capital. Thus, the Investment
Agreements can be legally performed. It should also be noted that the aforementioned
provision of the Company Law is a compulsory provision on administration. According to
Article 15 of the Guiding Opinions on Several Issues Concerning Trial of Cases on Disputes over
Civil and Commercial Contracts in the Current Situation and related jurisprudence, if a legal
provision clearly stipulates that a contract or act that violates the provision is invalid, then the
provision is usually a compulsory provision on validity, otherwise it is a compulsory provision
on administration. Besides, if the purpose of the legal provision is for administration, then it
is also a compulsory provision on administration. Article 142 of the Company Law does not
expressly stipulate that any contract or act that is in breach of the provision is invalid, and
it also sets out circumstances where the company is allowed to acquire its own shares. The
objective of the provision is to safeguard the principle of capital maintenance of the company
and to protect the interests of creditors. Therefore, the provision was drafted for administration
purposes. Accordingly, even if the provision of the Investment Agreements on the repurchase of

shares violates the provisions of Article 142 of the Company Law, such a contractual provision
is not necessarily null and void.
Second, the condition that triggers the repurchase obligation (the minimum profit guarantee) is
broadly defined as the investment value adjustment clause (commonly known as the "gambling
clause"). The validity of these terms differs depending on the subject of the repurchase
obligation. If the subject of the repurchase obligation are shareholders, it is generally
recognized as a valid clause. If the subject of the repurchase obligation is the target company,
its effectiveness is controversial. On the one hand, the Supreme People's Court found in Haifu
Investment and Gansu Shiheng Retrial Case (2012 Min Ti Zi No.11) that the minimum profit
guarantee reached between the investors and the target company will enable the investors to
obtain a relatively fixed profit, which was isolated from the actual performance of the target
company, thus damaging the interests of the company and the company's creditors. Therefore,
the gambling provision between the investor and the target company was invalid. On the
other hand, among the arbitration practice in China in recent years, some arbitral awards
that are well received by the industry hold that when the investor signs a capital increase
agreement with the target company, it has not yet become a target company shareholder. The
capital increase agreement forms the investment legal relationship. Therefore, the validity of
the gambling clause is subject to provisions of the Contract Law. An investor is regulated by the
Company Law only when it becomes a shareholder of the target company. The company and
its shareholders can conduct transactions, and it is inappropriate to regard the shareholders'
behavior of taking assets from the company as violations of the provisions of the Company Law
regarding principle of capital maintenance. It is very common for commercial parties to pay
shareholders according to the investment value adjustment mechanism. Reduction of assets
is not an impairment of interests. There is no necessary connection among the shareholder's
acquisition of the company's assets, violation of the principle of capital maintenance, and
capital reduction restrictions. Therefore, the gambling clause between the investor and the
target company is not per se invalid.
In view of the above differences in judicial practice and legal opinions, the emergency
arbitrator holds that the dispute between the Parties over the validity of Article 6.1 (10) of the
Investment Agreements is a core substantive dispute and should be put aside for the arbitral
tribunal to decide. However, in the emergency arbitration procedure, the dispute is insufficient
to deny the possibility of the Applicants' claim to prevail on the merits.
(2) Relationship between the listing obligation and the repurchase obligation:
First, the Respondents cited Article 16.6 of the Investment Agreements. The emergency

indemnity clause. Article 16.6 is a special arrangement on listing but not on repurchase.

The Respondents submitted that this request of the Applicants was a serious interference with the
Respondents' justifiable right of action. The Applicants' initiation of the arbitration procedure and
the emergency arbitration procedure in accordance with the arbitration clause stipulated in the
Investment Agreements shall not restrict the Respondents' justifiable right of action and access to
other justifiable reliefs.
The emergency arbitrator holds that: first, the fundamental procedural rights of the Respondents
should not be restricted or even deprived by the emergency arbitration procedure; second, if the
Respondents file a lawsuit, application, etc. in the courts of other jurisdictions, the legality and
justifiability of the act should be determined according to the law of that jurisdiction. Even if this
interim relief is granted by the emergency arbitrator, it would be difficult to be enforced by the court
of that jurisdiction; third, if the act of the Respondents to file a lawsuit, application or other
procedures objectively hinders the enforcement of the emergency arbitrator's decision, the
subsequent arbitral tribunal hearing the disputes between the Parties will decide whether the
Respondents' act has violated the good faith principle in arbitral proceedings; lastly, cooperation
with the decision or order of the emergency arbitrator is an inherent requirement of good faith
arbitration as applied in the issue of interim measures. The obligation to cooperate need not and is
inappropriate to be an independent interim measure. Therefore, this request of the Applicants is
not sustained.

The Applicants has signed preservation liability insurance with China Ping An Property Insurance

The Respondents submitted that the insurance totaling RMB 400 million provided by the Applicants

Cayman Islands Company Z should disclose in full any unfavorable circumstances it is subject to. If
information about the interim measures is disclosed, the stock price of the company will be
impacted, which would further damage the interests of the Respondents and the Applicants.
Moreover, once the interim measures are carried out, the capital chain of HK Capital Company M is
very likely to be affected, so are the daily operations of HK Capital Company M, Shenzhen Company
S and its affiliates in Mainland China. Therefore, the insurance amount of 400 million yuan is far
from being sufficient to cover the loss that the Respondents may suffer.
The Emergency Arbitrator holds that the request for an emergency arbitrator or arbitral tribunal to
grant interim measures is similar to applying for preservation measures in litigation. Since the
timing for requesting the designation of an emergency arbitrator and granting interim measures is
between the acceptance of the case and the constitution of the arbitral tribunal, reference could be
given to the rules of preservation in litigation (rather than pre-litigation preservation) in China in

with Article 100 of the Civil Procedure Law, the amount of the security shall not exceed 30% of the
claimed preservation amount; if the property involved in an application for preservation is the

DECISION
Based on the above opinions, the emergency arbitrator orders the following:
1.
The Respondents shall not transfer either with or without compensation, damage, pledge
or mortgage Cayman Islands Company Z's loan claims of HK$ 300 million to HK Capital
Company M, or otherwise create any encumbrance on such claims in any way;
2.
Mr. R shall not transfer either with or without compensation, damage, pledge or mortgage
the shares of HK Capital Company M, or otherwise create any encumbrance on such
claims in any way. Cayman Islands Company Z shall not transfer either with or without
compensation, damage, pledge or mortgage the shares of HK Capital Company M or
otherwise create any encumbrance other than to distribute the shares of HK Capital
Company M to each shareholder according to the shareholding ratio in compliance with
the Resolution of the Shareholders' Meeting on 18 September 2015;
3.
The Respondents shall not imply, encourage persons affiliated with Mr. R, directors,
officers, employees or agents of Cayman Islands Company Z to perform the restricted acts,
or let those persons to perform the restricted acts on the Respondents' behalf.
4.
The other requests for interim measures by the Applicants are overruled.
